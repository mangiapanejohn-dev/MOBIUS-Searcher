# A trained model of SOL's next move, and why it is not used (2026-10-04)

The question: can a model trained on price history say when to be in SOL,
well enough to pay for its trades? It was trained, tested the way the
[lab](LAB.md) tests a rule, and it did **not** clear the bar set before the
last look. It is in the lab as a paper rule, and nowhere else.

Everything here is reproduced by:

```bash
python3 scripts/direction_model.py fetch
python3 scripts/direction_model.py train
```

The run's output is [runs/direction-model-2026-10-04.txt](runs/direction-model-2026-10-04.txt),
the model and every number [runs/direction-model-2026-10-04.json](runs/direction-model-2026-10-04.json).

## What was done

- **Data.** OKX 15-minute candles of SOL-USDT and BTC-USDT from 2021-01-31
  to 2026-10-04: 198,899 bars each, no gap.
- **Inputs.** 26 numbers at each bar's close, from that bar and the ones
  before it only ([the list](LAB.md#a-trained-model-as-a-rule)). Checked on
  the real data: features recomputed for random bars from the past alone
  equal the ones used, to 2e-8.
- **Target.** Whether the price is higher 1 hour, 4 hours or 1 day after the
  next bar opens.
- **Models.** A logistic regression and boosted trees; six configurations
  with the three horizons. No tuning: one set of settings, written before the
  first run.
- **Rolling tests.** Train on two years, test on the three months after,
  move on three months: twelve tests, 2023-02-07 to 2026-02-03. The last
  labels of a training window end before its test begins.
- **Kept aside.** 2026-04-04 to 2026-10-04, looked at once, for the one
  configuration the rolling tests chose.
- **The rule a model makes.** In when its probability of a rise is in the top
  fifth of what it gave in training, out the horizon after the last such bar.
  23 USD, the lab's costs (1.15 bp of a trade and 6,366 lamports a
  transaction, each side).
- **The bar, set beforehand.** On the months kept aside the interval of what
  the rule adds a day lies above zero, and two thirds of the rolling tests
  are positive.

## The rolling tests

SOL rose 326 % over these three years.

| Model | Horizon | AUC | In SOL | Return | Same SOL | Rule adds a day [95 %] | Tests positive | Trades |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| logistic regression | 1 h | 0.532 | 36 % | +206 % | +126 % | +6.0 bp [−7.5, +19.2] | 7 of 12 | 4,219 |
| boosted trees | 1 h | 0.535 | 36 % | +338 % | +126 % | +8.8 bp [−3.1, +20.8] | 6 of 12 | 4,038 |
| logistic regression | 4 h | 0.533 | 47 % | +1,017 % | +168 % | +16.0 bp [+2.8, +28.7] | 9 of 12 | 1,094 |
| **boosted trees** | **4 h** | **0.537** | 50 % | +2,388 % | +181 % | **+23.1 bp [+10.3, +35.6]** | **11 of 12** | 1,171 |
| logistic regression | 1 d | 0.515 | 70 % | +876 % | +258 % | +11.7 bp [+0.1, +23.6] | 6 of 12 | 242 |
| boosted trees | 1 d | 0.528 | 66 % | +365 % | +244 % | +6.1 bp [−5.8, +18.1] | 5 of 12 | 258 |

`Same SOL` is a portfolio always holding the rule's average share of SOL;
`rule adds` is the return beyond it.

On these numbers alone the four-hour trees look like a find. Three things
temper that, and the last one decides it.

## What it had learned

**It buys after short falls, at certain hours.** The four-hour trees signal
on 19 % of bars. What those bars look like, against all bars of the rolling
tests:

| | When it signals | All bars |
|---|---|---|
| SOL up over the last hour | 31 % | 50 % |
| SOL up over the last day | 23 % | 50 % |
| close above its 1-day average | 26 % | 50 % |
| close above its 7-day average | 48 % | 50 % |
| in the bottom fifth of its 7-day range | 28 % | 16 % |
| in the top fifth of its 7-day range | 18 % | 23 % |

It signals most at 03–04 h and 17–19 h UTC (a quarter of those bars) and
least at 11–13 h (under a tenth).

**No rule of one line does the same.** Over the same three years:

| In for four hours after… | In SOL | Return | Same SOL | Rule adds a day [95 %] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SOL is down over the last day (buy the dip) | 65 % | +147 % | +239 % | −0.7 bp [−13.5, +12.1] |
| the close is in the bottom fifth of its 7-day range | 21 % | −43 % | +68 % | −7.8 bp [−18.8, +3.4] |
| the close is in the top fifth of its 7-day range | 29 % | +441 % | +96 % | +12.0 bp [−0.5, +24.6] |
| SOL is up over the last 3 days | 58 % | +542 % | +216 % | +9.7 bp [−2.9, +22.9] |

Buying every dip added nothing and buying the bottom of the week's range
lost; what the model does is narrower than either, and it cannot be checked
by a simpler rule.

**Some of it is the clock, and the measure is loose.** Trained on labels
shuffled by whole weeks (so that only the hour and the weekday still carry
anything), the four-hour regression shows +8.7 bp a day [−3.2, +21.4]; with
every feature a day old, +16.4 bp [+3.2, +29.8], as much as with fresh ones.
An interval that a model without the answers nearly clears is not a tight
measure of what a model with them adds.

**On the months kept aside it knew nothing.**

| 2026-04-04 to 2026-10-04 (SOL +50.2 %) | Boosted trees, 4 h |
|---|---|
| AUC | 0.499 |
| Return | +21.4 % |
| Deepest fall | −28.7 % |
| In SOL | 39 % |
| Same SOL | +19.6 % |
| Rule adds | +1.8 % |
| Rule adds a day [95 %] | +2.4 bp [−20.4, +28.7] |
| Trades, net of each | 141, +16.4 bp |

An AUC of a half is a coin. The interval contains zero: **not passed.** It is
also wide enough to contain the rolling tests' +23 bp, so six months neither
confirm the edge nor rule it out; they do not show it.

## What stands

- A model was trained and it predicts a little in the rolling tests (AUC
  0.53 to 0.54): mostly rebounds after short falls, and the time of day.
- Nothing here supports trading it. The one look the method allows did not
  pass: on the newest six months the same model was a coin.
- It runs in the lab as the `model` rule, where it stays out on every bar it
  was trained on; the lab's own backtest of those months agrees with the
  script (+21.6 % and 142 trades with a few hours more of candles, against
  +21.4 % and 141).

## What was not done

- No tuning of either model, no other features (funding, open interest,
  order flow), no other coin as the target. Each would be a new attempt and
  would have to be counted as one.
- No retraining on the months kept aside: the model in the file is the one
  that was judged.
